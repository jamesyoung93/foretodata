# Writing archive review

Review date: 2026-07-11

This non-public note records the editorial review of the 20 original blog-post PDFs supplied by James. The PDFs remain source material only. They were not copied into the public site because most are browser printouts with old navigation, newsletter forms, broken media frames, comments, or dated implementation details.

## Public archive strategy

Recent peer-reviewed and open-access research leads the homepage and Publications page. Writing is limited to three technical essays that reinforce the current scientific and analytical positioning.

### Selected technical essays

| Piece | Public treatment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Snap Tells and Offensive Predictability in the NFL | Retained and revised | Concrete feature engineering, classification, held-out AUC, and exploratory observational analysis. Causal language was removed. |
| Forecasting at Increasing Granularity | Reconstructed as clean HTML | Strong evidence of hierarchical forecasting judgment, reconciliation, compute constraints, and missing-versus-zero reasoning. Terminology and claims were updated. |
| From Pairwise to Batch Sequence Alignment in R | Retained and revised | Connects bioinformatics, pipeline scaling, measured runtime, local data handling, and a user-facing interface. Validation limits are now explicit. |

## Removed generated posts

Three 2026 guide-style Markdown posts were removed from the site:

- A Practical Guide to Causal Inference in ML
- Why Interpretable ML Wins in Business
- Lessons from Deploying LLMs in Production

Their repeated TL;DR structure, punchline fragments, generic certainty, and heavy dash usage did not sound like the original archive and weakened authorship credibility.

## Publication links

The homepage now leads with three recent open papers: the 2026 Scientific Reports FOX-gene paper, the 2026 AI Chemistry Rubisco paper, and the 2025 Metabolites paper. Publications uses descriptive labels such as Open paper, Open preprint, and Open dissertation rather than unlabeled arrow icons.
